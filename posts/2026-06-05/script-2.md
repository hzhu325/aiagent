## English Script (for CapCut international version)

November 14, 2004.
One hundred miles southwest of San Diego, California.
The USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group was running training exercises.
Then radar operators noticed something impossible.
Multiple objects were dropping from 80,000 feet — in under a second.
No aircraft on Earth can do that.
Commander David Fravor was scrambled to investigate.
Below him, the ocean churned violently — as if something massive lurked beneath the surface.
Then he saw it.
A white, oval-shaped object. Roughly 40 feet long.
No wings. No tail. No engine of any kind.
As Fravor maneuvered toward it — the craft accelerated and vanished.
Seconds later, radar reacquired it. More than 60 miles away.
A second flight crew captured it on infrared camera.
That footage became the famous FLIR video.
In 2020, the US Department of Defense officially declassified it.
In 2023, Fravor testified before Congress.
His words: "I've never seen anything like it — before or since."
What was that object?
Why was it hovering over a US nuclear carrier strike group?
